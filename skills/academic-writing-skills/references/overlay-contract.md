# Overlay Contract

## Purpose

Use an overlay to add context without changing the universal integrity core. An overlay may come from a domain skill, professor or reviewer rubric, journal guide, reporting standard, or project record.

## Required Classification

Classify every overlay rule as one of:

1. **design requirement:** follows from the declared study design
2. **domain check:** tests terminology, mechanisms, customary evidence, or field practice
3. **reviewer preference:** controls tone, structure, emphasis, or revision-round behavior
4. **venue requirement:** follows from an identified journal, conference, institution, or reporting standard
5. **project state:** supplies facts, locked wording, decisions, files, or prior-review status

Do not present categories 2–5 as universal scientific requirements.

## Precedence and Conflict Handling

Apply this precedence:

1. safety, ethics, and research-integrity constraints
2. verified evidence and authoritative project facts
3. explicit current user instructions
4. applicable venue or reporting requirements
5. study-design checks
6. domain conventions
7. reviewer preferences

When two sources at the same level conflict, report the conflict and identify what authority is needed. Do not silently choose the wording that sounds more polished.

## Safe Composition

Before applying an overlay:

- confirm the manuscript is within its scope
- load only relevant reference files
- state which rules are requirements and which are preferences
- identify any rule that would change locked meaning, add unsupported mechanism, or force an unsuitable article structure
- retain project-specific content outside the reusable skill

During review:

- use the universal issue severity and evidence-status model
- let the overlay calibrate attention, tone, and specialized checks
- preserve the manuscript archetype and study-design adapter
- require evidence for “why,” causal, validation, novelty, and feasibility claims

After review:

- report which overlay was applied
- identify conflicts, exceptions, and waivers
- include the functional-completeness retrospective

## Venue Profile

Before applying journal-specific advice, identify the target venue, **article type**, intended readers, publication stage, authoritative instructions/template, and **checked date**. Reuse an equivalent project record or a compact working note; do not require a new file for a local edit. Separate three questions:

- **Direction:** do the aims and scope, article genre, and audience match the actual question, evidence, and contribution? Describe fit without promising acceptance or inflating novelty.
- **Format:** which current requirements apply to this article type and stage: length, required sections, summaries, line numbering, editable tables, graphics, SM, data/software statements, and citation/reference style? Distinguish submission instructions from production conventions.
- **Style:** what reading order, explanatory depth, terminology, caption density, and visual organization serve these readers? A few recent comparable articles can illustrate practice; they do not override official instructions or become mandatory templates.

Identify the source and applicability of each consequential rule. Recheck when the venue, article type, stage, artifact, or relevant guidance changes, or the old profile is uncertain. A grammar-only repair can reuse unchanged verified rules without a new venue search. When guidance is unavailable or contradictory, state what remains unverified; continue safe local work without claiming journal compliance. Venue alignment does not authorize new analyses, invented results, reframing locked questions, or wholesale stylistic edits to an accepted version.

### WRR/ABM venue example, not a universal profile

Official sources checked **2026-10-03**; refresh relevant requirements before another submission:

- [WRR aims and scope](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/19447973/aims-and-scope/read-full-aims-and-scope) includes natural and social sciences of water and multiple research approaches. For the ABM study, ask how coupled household adaptation and flood-loss analysis advances understanding of water risk; naming an ABM or a basin alone does not answer that question or establish novelty.
- [AGU text and graphics requirements](https://www.agu.org/publications/authors/journals/text-graphics-requirements) supplies submission and companion-file rules. For example, citations in SM must also occur in the main text and main reference list; there is no separate SM bibliography under this guidance. Check the actual files rather than assuming the example manuscript complies.
- [AGU grammar and style guide](https://www.agu.org/publications/authors/journals/grammar-style-guide) uses an APA basis with AGU exceptions. Follow verified venue examples, not a generic APA edition or citation-manager label. The page mentions both a named older edition and the latest edition; resolve consequential ambiguity from current applicable instructions rather than silently inventing a version.

The ABM project's bold figure/table callouts and plain `to` connectors are author preferences, not established by these sources. Preserve the separation between that preference, the original examples, and journal requirements.

## Citation Support and Reference Format

Check two independent layers. **Support** asks whether the source actually substantiates the adjacent claim, at the stated population, time, scale, and causal strength. **Format/identity** asks whether the in-text key and bibliography refer to the right work in the active venue's style. Passing either layer does not prove the other.

For an added, removed, or affected citation, identify its role: background, evidence for a finding, a method origin, a data/software source, or a qualitative rationale for an assumption. Prefer the primary source for a specific finding, rule, method, or dataset; cite a review for its synthesis. Do not attribute a secondary source's cited finding to its own study, pad a sentence with merely topical references, or add an unrelated citation just to retain a bibliography entry. Read enough of the source to check its methods, scope, result, and relevant qualifications; an abstract, title, or DOI resolves identity but may not establish claim support. An inaccessible source stays unverified, not approved.

At reference reconciliation, check authors/order, group-author spelling and acronym introduction, year and letter suffixes, title, outlet, volume/issue, pages versus article number, DOI/URL, source type, version, and retrieval date where applicable. Distinguish publication year from retrieval year; verify preprint versus published status without silently substituting a new work. Apply same-year disambiguation to the actual author lists and venue rules, not every entry sharing a first author. Check citations in prose, tables, captions, notes, and SM against the required reference list(s), and inspect uncited entries in context before removal. A dataset citation and an availability statement serve different functions.

### ABM citation examples (source and teaching boundary)

These passages/entries come from the same clean ABM source identified in the display guide. They are historical examples, not a certificate that its entire reference list is correct.

**Original TP passage:**

> At the beginning of each year, household TP is updated using the tract-level GUL ratio from the previous year. This update assumes that household TP responds to flood experiences in the surrounding community, rather than flood damage only to the household’s own property (Mondino et al., 2020).

**Original reference:** Mondino, E., Scolobig, A., Borga, M., & Di Baldassarre, G. (2020). The role of experience and different sources of knowledge in shaping flood risk awareness. Water, 12(8), Article 2130. https://doi.org/10.3390/w12082130

**Teaching boundary:** the [primary paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/8/2130/html) examines experience and knowledge sources in an Italian municipality. This is contextual rationale, not validation of the ABM's tract-level proxy, individual TP update, or parameter values. Table 1 likewise distinguishes cited rationales from the exact thresholds selected by the modeler. Do not turn a supporting citation into evidence that those settings were measured or validated.

**Original dataset entry:** FEMA. (2026b). NFIP redacted claims (Version 3) [Data set]. OpenFEMA. Retrieved August 13, 2026, from https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3

**Teaching boundary:** this entry identifies a dataset and version rather than just an agency homepage. Preserve verified identity and distinguish it from the manuscript's other FEMA source keyed `2026a`; propagate `2026a/2026b` consistently after the active venue's disambiguation rule is checked. Do not assume the year/suffix/group-author rendering is correct merely because it appears in this source. Verify the actual dataset metadata and usage before adapting it; do not invent an access date or substitute a new version. These examples do not prescribe APA formatting for a different venue.

## Reviewer-Round Overlays

Treat review round as maturity, not as a guarantee that earlier issues are resolved. Compare current files with prior comments when available. Do not infer progress from comment count alone.

In later rounds, prioritize unresolved blockers and regression from new edits. Avoid inventing new discretionary concerns when the manuscript is stable, but never ignore a newly discovered validity or ethics issue merely because the round is late.

## Prohibited Overlay Behavior

Do not let an overlay:

- require numbered questions, an IMRAD sequence, or a public repository for every paper
- force a mechanism into Results without evidence
- treat one professor's wording preference as universal English
- infer facts or authorship roles from formatting
- conceal a method limitation to match a preferred narrative
- replace a formal statistical comparison with visual similarity
- carry project facts into another manuscript
