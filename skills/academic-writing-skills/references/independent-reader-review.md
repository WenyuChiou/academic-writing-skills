# Independent Reader Review: Prerequisites and Progression

Load this guide for an explicit independent readability review, a substantive
section with several dependent concepts, or a repeated comprehension failure.
Use it to find where a reader loses the thread, not to replace scientific review
or enforce simpler synonyms. A grammar-only sentence edit uses the local reader
walkthrough; it does not automatically require agents or a whole-paper audit.

## Choose scope and readers

- Freeze the exact candidate, audience, authorized review unit, and included
  figures/captions. Use stable paragraph/sentence locators and a file hash when
  available. Both readers review the **same complete unit**, in order; do not
  split terminology into one half and flow into the other.
- Use two independent cold readers when tools and policy permit. The concept
  reader prioritizes quantities, terms, referents, and missing prerequisites.
  The flow reader prioritizes sentence/paragraph progression and a source-grounded
  account of what each paragraph establishes. Both still check comprehension.
- Add a third precision reader only when a repair risks changing a construct,
  denominator, uncertainty, or inferential strength, or a substantive disagreement
  needs examination. This reader may consult the supplied source/meaning contract
  and proposed repairs; do not describe this informed check as a blind first read.
- There is **no fixed model**, provider, concurrency, or required round count.
  Follow the host's current delegation and budget rules. If independent execution
  is unavailable, label a single-agent self-check as such, not as two reviewers.

These roles simulate informed researchers from outside the immediate specialty.
Multiple AI roles, even with different personas, are **not genuine human-reader
evidence**, proven disciplinary diversity, or a guarantee that everyone will
understand the paper.

## Give cold readers only the reading context

Supply the intended audience, review scope, frozen text, and displays needed to
read it, **without author explanations**, earlier drafting history, hidden answer
keys, expected defect locations, or another reader's report. Do not label a
passage as the bad example. If sources are needed to judge a proposed repair,
use the separate precision check rather than priming the cold reading.

For a whole-paper review, read from the beginning through the end of the agreed
scope, including affected captions. For a section or paragraph review, supply
available preceding context or a verified list of earlier definitions when that
is how the intended reader encounters it. State missing context as a limit; do
not claim a full-paper review of an excerpt. An Abstract, standalone caption,
or independent response has its own reading boundary.

## Read at the point of need

For each sentence, ask what the text has established **before this first-use
boundary**, what is new, and what lets the reader connect them. When the current
claim requires a meaning that has not been supplied, keep that obstruction
distinct from a **later definition** that resolves it. A later definition does
not retroactively repair a required first-use meaning; this is not a requirement
to front-load every operational detail.

Check especially:

1. **A new label for familiar material.** If insurance and moving have been
   described, does `pathway` name an option, a sequence, or a mechanism? Make the
   connection explicit using confirmed meaning; do not ban the word itself.
2. **A name without a role.** Expanding an acronym does not explain what a score
   measures, how it is constructed, or what a larger value means. Explain only
   what the current claim needs. Check the expanded name and the study-specific
   meaning separately: can the reader explain what is being measured without
   supplying their own specialist definition? Do not invent a formula or direction.
3. **A changed comparison.** Does a loss share now use income rather than total
   loss? Have the population, scale, aggregation, time, or reference condition
   changed without notice? Preserve numbers, units, and inference limits.
4. **A missing link or referent.** What does `this difference` refer to when two
   comparisons precede it? Does the next sentence introduce a new concept or
   mechanism without explaining its relationship to the reported evidence?

Necessary technical terms may remain. Prefer a short definition or mapping
before first use, a concrete subject and verb, or an explicit comparator over
synonym rotation, noun stacks, or textbook digressions. Do not require every
basic word to be defined for an informed reader.

## Calibrate before recommending edits

Apply these decisions to the supplied reading unit, not to a preferred paper
template. Before keeping a finding:

- **Test necessity now.** Identify what the reader must know to understand the
  current claim, not everything a specialist could ask about the method. An
  Introduction can introduce a concept and purpose while Methods later provides
  sampling, cutoffs, or equations. A symbol introduced in an equation and explained
  immediately in its `where` clause is not automatically a first-use defect. Flag
  deferred detail only when its absence prevents the present inference or hides
  a changed comparison.
- **Check the available context.** Re-read preceding definitions, neighboring
  sentences, and stated caveats before calling something absent. For a standalone
  display, assess the caption together with the figure, axes, legend, or table
  notes. Do not demand duplicate expansions when that combined entry point already
  supplies the necessary meaning. If a display or supplement is unavailable, say
  so; its absence from the packet does not prove an omission in the manuscript.
- **Respect the reader function and research design.** An extension analysis may
  belong in Discussion when its local design is explained before its findings.
  Conceptual arguments, qualitative accounts, and evidence syntheses need their
  own prerequisites, not compulsory study-area sections or experimental results.
  Recommend relocation only for an actual dependency or an applicable requirement,
  not because content differs from an IMRAD template.
- **Separate observation from suspicion.** Distinguish a supported obstruction,
  a source-dependent question, an optional preference, and no change needed.
  A suspect plotted statistic, unstated selection rule, or possible citation
  mismatch requires a located question and the source needed to settle it. Do not
  infer invalid sampling, selective reporting, or a wrong mean/median label from
  unfamiliar wording or a cropped image alone. Different summaries and tests can
  legitimately use different statistics when their roles are clear.
- **Choose the least invasive action.** Retain wording when it works; otherwise
  name an actor or comparator, add a short mapping, or move an existing definition
  before considering broader restructuring. Request missing facts instead of
  supplying them. A readability finding does not authorize changed results, new
  analyses, or global replacements. Keep codes and technical terms when useful,
  but provide their relationship to the quantity or comparison being interpreted.

Set severity by the consequence for comprehension or inference, not by how
unfamiliar a term looks. Consolidate repeated symptoms of one missing prerequisite;
do not multiply defects or seek a quota of findings. Record no-change outcomes as
well as concerns. A reviewer preference may be followed when authorized without
being presented as a universal correctness rule.

## Return located findings, not a fluency verdict

For each substantive obstruction, record:

- **Location and wording:** paragraph/sentence or caption, plus the exact phrase.
- **Classification:** supported obstruction, source-dependent question, optional
  preference, or no change needed; distinguish required repair from optional advice.
- **Missing prerequisite:** the concept, comparison, actor, referent, or relation
  not supplied at that point; identify any later definition separately.
- **Consequence:** what the reader cannot decide or might misinterpret.
- **Smallest repair:** move a supplied definition, map a label to earlier content,
  name a referent, or ask for a missing fact. Mark conditional wording explicitly.
- **Evidence needed:** for an unresolved source-dependent question, identify what
  would settle it and withhold an asserted correction until it is available.

Add a brief, source-grounded paraphrase of each paragraph's contribution and
how it follows from the previous paragraph. For a Results paragraph, identify the
observed pattern and what it establishes for the stated question, not just repeat
the values or variable list. If that meaning is already clear, record no change;
do not require a stock so-what sentence, explicit SQ label, or untested mechanism.
This does not add an interpretation requirement to a decoding caption or a local
grammar task. Say `unclear` when a necessary meaning cannot be recovered from the
supplied text. Do not fill gaps using a model's specialist knowledge
and then declare the passage self-contained. Mark paragraphs without an issue
too, so a report of a few findings does not imply coverage of unread material.
Use a compact table or equivalent record when the unit is large; return the
requested concise summary without discarding the located findings.

## Reconcile, repair, and recheck

The parent checks each finding against the exact text and intended audience.
**Do not use majority voting**: one supported missing prerequisite remains a
finding even if the other reader missed it. Conversely, unfamiliarity alone is
not enough to replace an established term that is already explained at that
reading boundary. Record resolved, retained-with-reason, or unresolved outcomes.

Do not invent results, definitions, citations, or causal links to make the prose
flow. Preserve scientific meaning and locked wording; revise only when authorized.
Recheck each repair and its affected neighbors, including newly introduced terms.
Re-read the complete unit if structure or prerequisite order changes across it.
Keep retries bounded by the host policy; unresolved barriers remain visible.
Report which readers actually ran, scope covered, located findings and open
limits. A static registration test or fluent paraphrase is not evidence that an
independent reading occurred or that scientific validity was verified.

## Worked scenarios

The passages below are hypothetical teaching cases, not verified ABM results or
universal terminology bans. Each minimal repair uses only the stated meaning.
For a blind test, keep these examples and answer keys outside the reader packet;
test additional passages rather than recall of these corrections.

### 1. An abstract label arrives after concrete actions

- **Problem:** `Households can buy insurance or move. The pathways differ by tenure.`
- **Trigger:** first use of `pathways` after only actions have been named.
- **Judgment:** the reader cannot tell whether this means available options or
  their sequence over time. Suppose the author confirms it means available options.
- **Minimal repair:** `The adaptation options available to homeowners and renters
  differ.` If a temporal sequence is intended, name that sequence instead.
- **Boundary:** retain a defined `adaptation pathway` when sequence is the actual
  construct. A new, unneeded label may be removed rather than formally defined.

### 2. The definition is present, but too late

- **Problem:** `Functional redundancy was higher at site A. Functional redundancy
  means that several species perform the same ecological function.`
- **Trigger:** the result requires the technical meaning before it is introduced.
- **Judgment:** the later definition supplies the missing fact but not in time.
- **Minimal repair:** place the supplied definition before the site comparison.
- **Boundary:** do not redefine the term if preceding in-scope text already defines
  it. Do not infer the ecological function or a measurement method from the name.

### 3. The acronym is expanded, but the quantity is unknown

- **Problem:** `We calculated the Community Recovery Index (CRI). CRI increased.`
- **Trigger:** a score is interpreted with only its expanded name available.
- **Judgment:** the reader does not know the measured recovery dimension or what
  an increase indicates. The formula and direction are not supplied.
- **Minimal repair:** request that definition and direction; add a short verified
  explanation before interpreting the change. Do not invent them from `recovery`.
- **Boundary:** the current result may need a meaning and direction, not the entire
  formula. Full construction can remain in Methods with a relevant pointer.

### 4. A percentage silently changes its denominator

- **Problem:** `Insurance covered 40% of property loss. Protection was 8% after
  considering income.` The second measure is loss covered divided by annual income.
- **Trigger:** `protection` appears to be the same percentage but uses a new basis.
- **Judgment:** the text may imply coverage fell rather than a different comparison.
- **Minimal repair:** `The loss covered by insurance equaled 8% of annual income.`
- **Boundary:** keep both denominators and the supplied values; do not claim greater
  welfare, affordability, or causal protection solely from this normalization.

### 5. Several comparisons precede an unnamed difference

- **Problem:** `Losses were higher for owners. Insurance enrollment was higher for
  renters. This difference motivates further analysis.`
- **Trigger:** a demonstrative follows two possible comparisons.
- **Judgment:** the intended comparison is not uniquely identified.
- **Minimal repair:** if confirmed, `The difference in insurance enrollment...`;
  otherwise ask which comparison the author means.
- **Boundary:** do not replace every `this` or `these`; clear local referents are useful.

### 6. The caption is an independent entry point

- **Problem:** a standalone figure caption says only `AEP and the margin by tenure`.
  The text elsewhere defines AEP as aggregate exceedance probability and the margin
  as the difference between two named loss curves at the same probability.
- **Trigger:** the figure must be interpretable without locating body definitions.
- **Judgment:** the caption lacks the quantities and mapping needed to decode it.
- **Minimal repair:** give the full term, identify the loss curves, and state the
  supplied margin definition and unit/normalization relevant to that display.
- **Boundary:** use the existing [display guide](figures-tables-and-supplements.md);
  assess the caption with its display and legend before adding text. Do not
  duplicate available decoding information or create a second caption rule system.

### 7. Defined technical language is a positive control

- **Problem tested:** `Species richness is the number of species observed in a
  plot. Richness was higher in shaded plots than in unshaded plots.`
- **Trigger:** review whether the mechanism overflags an unfamiliar-looking term.
- **Judgment:** concept and comparison are already available at the point of use.
- **Minimal repair:** none needed for terminology. This wording reports a comparison,
  not a demonstrated effect of shade; preserve that distinction.
- **Boundary:** do not add another definition, remove the construct, or certify
  unrelated sampling and causal validity from this short passage.

### 8. A local correction does not expand the task

- **Problem:** the user requests only `The samples was stored at 4°C.` be corrected.
- **Trigger:** a grammar-only task with neighboring content locked.
- **Judgment:** change `was` to `were`, keeping temperature and meaning.
- **Minimal repair:** `The samples were stored at 4°C.` No independent whole-section
  review is required by this guide.
- **Boundary:** if a local meaning is unresolved, ask only for necessary context;
  do not invent procedures or claim that the whole manuscript was checked.
