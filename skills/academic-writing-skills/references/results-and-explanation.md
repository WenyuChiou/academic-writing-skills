## Scope and Evidence Order

Use this guide for planning, drafting, or substantively reviewing findings, including synthesis findings, propositions, and benchmark evaluations under other headings. Preserve functioning submitted or advisor-edited text; repair the missing function rather than rewriting the section wholesale.

Once the reader knows what is being compared, organize a result unit around **finding → necessary evidence → meaning → substantive answer**. Supply any missing display orientation first, as described below. These are information functions, not four mandatory sentences. State what was found, identify the comparison, report enough evidence to judge it, and explain what it establishes about the question. Put reproducible procedures in Methods; refer back briefly when a design detail is needed to understand a finding.

Choose the main pattern or comparison before arranging the supporting values. Do not mechanically enumerate variables, groups, or panels: the reader should know what the values demonstrate, rather than have to infer the finding from a numerical list. Variable, panel, or chronological order is appropriate when it advances a clear analytical argument. A finding can be heterogeneous, uncertain, or show no clear change; pattern-first writing does not authorize a stronger claim or an invented mechanism.

Give the “so what” where the paragraph or subsection completes its analytical point. It can identify a pathway contrast, trade-off, boundary, supported relationship, or negative finding. Do not end with only statistics, “These results answer SQ1,” or a vague declaration of importance. Temporarily remove the question label and introductory metadiscourse: the answer must remain intelligible. Numbered SQ/RQ labels are optional navigation, not evidence.

Test what the explanation adds. Decoding a score or coefficient can be necessary
at first use and can fully answer a request for its meaning. If the question asks
what a comparison teaches us, decoding the same observation again is insufficient:
identify the supported distinction, consequence or boundary learned from that
comparison. Read the relevant evidence together before adding another sentence.
A match in one dimension need not reproduce the reference on another; for example,
matching one group's spread can coexist with a different between-group pattern.
Retain functioning explanations and definitions rather than demanding a new
mechanism or a closing formula after each number.

Build that explanation in coherent result units, rather than postponing all meaning until an omnibus closing sentence. Several values can support one finding: keep them together, explain what that comparison establishes, then let the next unit deepen, contrast with, or qualify it. For example, establish what score levels mean before changing to dispersion, and distinguish individual relationships from a whole-model comparison before drawing a group-level conclusion. The required bridge is the analytical relation, not another connector. Do not insert an interpretation after every number, repeat the question at every paragraph, or split a functioning multi-value finding.

### Plan the joint answer before rewriting

When displays or subsections jointly address a question in scope, map the
question to the verified finding and limit of each display or subsection before
polishing any of them. State what none answers alone, the supported joint answer,
and what it changes for the study's substantive interpretation. Keep this plan
in the existing writing contract or a brief note; it need not appear verbatim
in the manuscript. If a required layer is unavailable, leave the joint answer
open rather than inferring it from the available layer. A local wording edit
uses only affected context and does not trigger a section rewrite.

After drafting, recover the joint answer from the prose with question labels
and stock framing removed. "This is the first level" does not explain the
relationship. In a hypothetical example, wider group mean separation can coexist with less
within-group spread and smaller descriptive group differences in fitted paths.
These answer different questions about group averages, variation and relationships;
they establish neither equal paths nor a shared internal rule. Keep several
values together when they support one finding; no fixed paragraph template applies.

Distinguish the current layer's finding from its relevance to later interpretation.
For example, different measurement inputs can contextualize a score comparison
without explaining every score difference or prohibiting a subsequent relationship
comparison. State only the consequence supported by the supplied design. Keep
evidence and its explanation together; place technical table pointers where they
help rather than interrupt that unit. Use [evidence-context case 11](evidence-context-cases.md#11-comparison-contribution-is-not-a-sequence-label)
when this particular execution gap recurs; retain functioning prose unchanged.

At section closure, read the layers jointly: what becomes clear only when their
findings are combined? Distinguish score levels, within-group spread, group gaps
and relationships when those are the actual comparison dimensions. Preserve the
author's reference direction and test level; a descriptive contrast or separate
within-source p-values do not establish a tested difference between sources.
If the joint contribution is already explicit, retain it without a new summary.

## Visuals and Substantive Answers

Prefer one primary question or analytical purpose per figure or table. This is not a mandatory one-figure/one-question ratio: related panels can support one cumulative comparison, and several visuals can jointly answer a question. Split unrelated panel groups when their different purposes obscure the argument; do not multiply figures merely to comply with a ratio.

Before a display, supply the question or comparison when the context needs it. After the necessary evidence, state the answer at the natural unit boundary. Do not repeat a full preview and synthesis around every panel. Trace values to the actual outputs and displays, checking baseline, group, time, units, denominator, aggregation, summary statistic, uncertainty, and rounding. Displayed rounded endpoints need not reproduce a percentage calculated from unrounded values; verify the underlying calculation and explain rounding when material rather than changing values to force agreement.

### First and Repeated Display Encounters

At the first encounter with an unfamiliar display or comparison, briefly tell the
reader what evidence it shows, which entities or conditions are compared, and
what to look for before reporting values. For a distribution, distinguish scores
or measurements, the population, and whether the comparison concerns location,
spread, or shape. Explain a nonobvious encoding only when needed for that finding;
leave the full legend and plotting procedure in the caption and Methods. The
orientation may already be supplied by adjacent prose; do not add a duplicate.

Then state the observed pattern, support it with the relevant values, and explain
its supported meaning at the paragraph or subsection's natural closure. "Figure X
presents the results" alone does not orient a reader, but neither a figure-first
opening nor the verb "presents" is inherently wrong when its subject and purpose
are already clear. Do not replace a useful finding-led opening with metadiscourse.
In the explanation, name whose values vary and at what level; a phrase such as
"less variation in the summaries" can obscure variation among people, sites, or
repeated runs. Concision must preserve that distinction.

For later displays using the same established reading logic, carry that context
forward and introduce only the new population, condition, comparison, or finding.
Do not repeat the plot tutorial, legend, or "we first / we then present" frame for
each figure. Reintroduce meaning when the quantity, denominator, population,
encoding, or uncertainty changes, or when the unit must stand alone. A shared
plot type does not establish a shared estimand. Apply the same decision to tables,
maps, qualitative displays, and evidence syntheses rather than assuming every
Results section contains distribution plots.

Use [figures-tables-and-supplements.md](figures-tables-and-supplements.md) for caption, note, visual-size, and synchronization checks.

## Mechanisms and Logical Flow

### Result–explanation continuity

Read a result and its explanation as continuous prose, not as separately polished
sentences. At their boundary, identify the specific observed comparison that the
explanation interprets and what that interpretation adds. A sequence of correct
short sentences can still read like disconnected notes when it changes subject,
comparison, denominator or analytical level without supplying that relation.

Repair that relation with the smallest change: name the result being explained,
put a necessary comparison beside it, reorder a premise, or join clauses when
their contrast, condition or qualification belongs together. Do not join every
short sentence, impose a minimum length, or add a stock connector. Preserve clear
short prose and technical distinctions; if the relation needs unavailable evidence,
state that limit rather than inventing a cause to make the passage flow.

For example, given only similar group means and a smaller between-person spread
in group A, `The means were similar. A was concentrated. The groups agreed.`
does not communicate the comparison safely. A minimal repair is `The groups had
similar mean scores, but responses varied less among people in group A.` This
connects the supplied findings without claiming matched agreement or equivalence.
It is a hypothetical illustration, not a rule to merge all results into one sentence.

Separate a model or procedural rule, the observed output, an explanation supported by decomposition or another appropriate analysis, and a plausible but untested interpretation. A rule alone does not establish why an aggregate pattern occurred. A citation supporting a real-world mechanism does not demonstrate its operation in the present dataset or simulation.

A hedge such as “may” or “consistent with” does not supply missing evidence.
For a requested why, state the supported comparative meaning, then separate any
plausible explanation from the evidence needed to assess it. An on-topic citation
must support the particular explanation attached to it; a prior theory or general
association does not establish this model's training data, internal rule or cause.
Leave that causal question open when the supplied evidence cannot answer it.

Treat an advisor, reviewer or external AI suggestion as a proposed interpretation, not a
verified numerical or mechanistic source. Before adopting it, match the statistic,
variable pair, score/item definition, model version and run set to the current
output. Separate the observed pattern, its study-level meaning, a proposed
mechanism and the test needed for that mechanism; keep independently supported
synthesis even if a proposed explanation or statistic fails verification.

An unavailable test is not evidence that it was never run. When comparing maxima, check whether they refer to the same component before drawing a component-level conclusion.

Connect explanation to the reported pattern through an explicit relation. “A therefore B” requires evidence for that link, not just plausible sequencing. If an explanation is untested, qualify it and place its extended interpretation in Discussion when the genre separates Results and Discussion. A concise Results qualification can prevent a misleading reading without becoming a speculative causal narrative.

Do not infer individual change from a group mean, isolate one cause from several simultaneous changes, or turn scenario mechanics into empirical validation. Name the contribution actually demonstrated and the diagnostic still needed for a stronger claim. Do not run that diagnostic merely because a prose review exposed the gap.

## Hidden Comparison Checks

At chapter closure, select applicable questions using [lifecycle-and-routing.md](lifecycle-and-routing.md). Do not impose every numerical check on every research design.

| Content trigger | Check before accepting the interpretation |
|---|---|
| Changing group averages or trajectories | Could membership, relocation, attrition, reclassification, or weights change the composition? Distinguish within-unit change from composition where evidence permits. |
| Rates, reductions, or normalization | Is the reference group/scenario/time named? Are numerator, denominator, weighting, aggregation order, and initial/current population clear? Equal percent units can conceal different meanings. |
| Ranked curves or quantile differences | Are values compared at the same probability, or for the same observation/event? Do not call a difference of separately ranked curves an event-matched payout or treatment effect. |
| Replications, error bars, or intervals | What uncertainty is represented: stochastic runs, measurement, sampling, parameters, or sequences? Do not convert conditional simulation variability into coverage of all uncertainties. |
| Spatial categories or changed colors | Do colors represent levels, changes, or joint categories? A category boundary can conceal a small nonzero change; a color change is not by itself evidence of a new mechanism or reversal of the main conclusion. |
| Similar-looking outcomes | Is similarity descriptive, or was equivalence/agreement tested with defined criteria? Do not invent a formal test. |
| Scores from different constructs on a shared scale | Does the stated benchmark support the interpretation of each score? A common range or midpoint does not establish which construct is more important, stronger, or more influential. Descriptive values can remain when their scoring meanings are clear. |
| Component tests followed by a group or model claim | Does evidence test that exact contrast and level? Separate within-group significance cannot establish a between-group difference; several nonsignificant components cannot establish whole-model equality. Preserve a central negative finding without upgrading it to equivalence. |
| Statistical significance followed by an importance claim | Is practical importance supported by magnitude, uncertainty, a relevant benchmark, and study context? A small p-value alone supplies none of these. |
| Qualitative themes or synthesis counts | Are counts illustrations, coded occurrences, or prevalence estimates? Retain negative cases and evidence boundaries; do not promote frequency to population generality. |
| Theorem or framework application | Which premise or requirement supports the conclusion? A demonstration does not establish every claimed boundary condition or broad utility. |

For example, a declining perception mean in a changing neighborhood sample can reflect lower individual values, changed membership, or both. Without unit-following evidence, report the aggregate decline and leave its decomposition unresolved. This is a hypothetical diagnostic, not a prescribed explanation for any paper.

## Reader Test

Read the result unit with the visual at its intended size. Can a reader explain what was compared, what changed, how the evidence supports the explanation, and why the finding matters for the question? If not, repair the specific missing link. Read opening and closing sentences in sequence to test cumulative logic, not compliance with a stock sentence pattern. Report unavailable evidence rather than certifying unseen outputs.
