## Scope and Evidence Order

Use this guide for planning, drafting, or substantively reviewing findings, including synthesis findings, propositions, and benchmark evaluations under other headings. Preserve functioning submitted or advisor-edited text; repair the missing function rather than rewriting the section wholesale.

Organize a result unit around **finding → necessary evidence → meaning → substantive answer**. These are information functions, not four mandatory sentences. State what was found, identify the comparison, report enough evidence to judge it, and explain what it establishes about the question. Put reproducible procedures in Methods; refer back briefly when a design detail is needed to understand a finding.

Choose the main pattern or comparison before arranging the supporting values. Do not mechanically enumerate variables, groups, or panels: the reader should know what the values demonstrate, rather than have to infer the finding from a numerical list. Variable, panel, or chronological order is appropriate when it advances a clear analytical argument. A finding can be heterogeneous, uncertain, or show no clear change; pattern-first writing does not authorize a stronger claim or an invented mechanism.

Give the “so what” where the paragraph or subsection completes its analytical point. It can identify a pathway contrast, trade-off, boundary, supported relationship, or negative finding. Do not end with only statistics, “These results answer SQ1,” or a vague declaration of importance. Temporarily remove the question label and introductory metadiscourse: the answer must remain intelligible. Numbered SQ/RQ labels are optional navigation, not evidence.

## Visuals and Substantive Answers

Prefer one primary question or analytical purpose per figure or table. This is not a mandatory one-figure/one-question ratio: related panels can support one cumulative comparison, and several visuals can jointly answer a question. Split unrelated panel groups when their different purposes obscure the argument; do not multiply figures merely to comply with a ratio.

Before a display, supply the question or comparison when the context needs it. After the necessary evidence, state the answer at the natural unit boundary. Do not repeat a full preview and synthesis around every panel. Trace values to the actual outputs and displays, checking baseline, group, time, units, denominator, aggregation, summary statistic, uncertainty, and rounding. Displayed rounded endpoints need not reproduce a percentage calculated from unrounded values; verify the underlying calculation and explain rounding when material rather than changing values to force agreement.

Use [figures-tables-and-supplements.md](figures-tables-and-supplements.md) for caption, note, visual-size, and synchronization checks.

## Mechanisms and Logical Flow

Separate a model or procedural rule, the observed output, an explanation supported by decomposition or another appropriate analysis, and a plausible but untested interpretation. A rule alone does not establish why an aggregate pattern occurred. A citation supporting a real-world mechanism does not demonstrate its operation in the present dataset or simulation.

Connect explanation to the reported pattern through an explicit relation. “A therefore B” requires evidence for that link, not just plausible sequencing. If an explanation is untested, qualify it and place its extended interpretation in Discussion when the genre separates Results and Discussion. A concise Results qualification can prevent a misleading reading without becoming a speculative causal narrative.

Do not infer individual change from a group mean, isolate one cause from several simultaneous changes, or turn scenario mechanics into empirical validation. Name the contribution actually demonstrated and the diagnostic still needed for a stronger claim. Do not run that diagnostic merely because a prose review exposed the gap.

## Hidden Comparison Checks

At chapter closure, select applicable questions using [lifecycle-and-routing.md](lifecycle-and-routing.md). Do not impose every numerical check on every research design.

| Content trigger | Check before accepting the interpretation |
|---|---|
| Changing group averages or trajectories | Could membership, relocation, attrition, reclassification, or weights change the composition? Distinguish within-unit change from composition where evidence permits. |
| Rates, reductions, or normalization | Is the reference group/scenario/time named? Are numerator, denominator, weighting, aggregation order, and initial/current population clear? Equal percent units can conceal different meanings. |
| Ranked curves or quantile differences | Are values compared at the same probability, or for the same observation/event? Do not call a difference of separately ranked curves an event-matched payout or treatment effect. |
| Replications, error bars, or intervals | What uncertainty is represented: stochastic runs, measurement, sampling, parameters, or sequences? Do not convert conditional simulation variability into coverage of all uncertainties. |
| Spatial categories or changed colors | Do colors represent levels, changes, or joint categories? A category boundary can conceal a small nonzero change; a color change is not by itself evidence of a new mechanism or reversal of the main conclusion. |
| Similar-looking outcomes | Is similarity descriptive, or was equivalence/agreement tested with defined criteria? Do not invent a formal test. |
| Qualitative themes or synthesis counts | Are counts illustrations, coded occurrences, or prevalence estimates? Retain negative cases and evidence boundaries; do not promote frequency to population generality. |
| Theorem or framework application | Which premise or requirement supports the conclusion? A demonstration does not establish every claimed boundary condition or broad utility. |

For example, a declining perception mean in a changing neighborhood sample can reflect lower individual values, changed membership, or both. Without unit-following evidence, report the aggregate decline and leave its decomposition unresolved. This is a hypothetical diagnostic, not a prescribed explanation for any paper.

## Reader Test

Read the result unit with the visual at its intended size. Can a reader explain what was compared, what changed, how the evidence supports the explanation, and why the finding matters for the question? If not, repair the specific missing link. Read opening and closing sentences in sequence to test cumulative logic, not compliance with a stock sentence pattern. Report unavailable evidence rather than certifying unseen outputs.
