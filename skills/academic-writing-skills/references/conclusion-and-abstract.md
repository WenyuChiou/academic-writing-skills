## Conclusion

Synthesize **what the study did → what it established → what that adds and where it applies**. A direct opening such as “This study examined…” is valid. Use “aims to” when it accurately states purpose; report completed work with an appropriate tense. Do not require a new importance–gap–purpose introduction before identifying the study.

State both the method and research task, the subject or system, and relevant place, time, or analytical scope. Naming a framework is not the same as explaining what it was used to investigate. Preserve the actual task verb and distinguish developing a method, demonstrating an application, comparing groups, and validating a prediction.

Synthesize answers in question or analytical order rather than replaying each figure. Retain the main contrast, comparator, direction, and any condition essential to meaning. Describe a mechanism only at the strength supported by the analysis. Use the main Results as the evidentiary basis and Discussion to qualify them; do not introduce a new finding or promote an extension into the headline merely because it sounds broader.

End with a concrete contribution, implication, or use boundary justified by those answers. Policy feasibility and affordability claims need relevant evidence; a comparison of losses alone does not establish them. No fixed paragraph count is required. Do not copy the Abstract, force a grand recommendation, or equate the number of available options with their actual effectiveness.

## Abstract Identity and Information Functions

Rebuild the Abstract from the stabilized manuscript, not an outdated synopsis. Make the study understandable without its figures, internal codes, or drafting history. Supply these information functions at the density appropriate to the genre and verified venue limits:

| Function | What the reader should learn |
|---|---|
| Problem or scientific need | What understanding or decision matters, and what relevant knowledge is missing. |
| Purpose | What the study investigates, develops, estimates, compares, or establishes. |
| Method and its role | What approach produces the evidence and what that approach actually does. Avoid technique names without purpose. |
| Subject and scope | Who or what is studied and the location, population, dataset, time period, or setting needed to interpret the answer. |
| Main findings | The supported substantive answers, named comparison, direction or magnitude, and essential qualifications. |
| Contribution and boundary | What understanding is added and the scope in which that claim is justified. |

These are information functions, not six mandatory blocks or fixed sentence counts. Combine them when natural, or follow a verified structured-abstract format. Study context is not automatically a geographic testbed: it can be the substantive subject, a selected sample, a theoretical domain, or an evaluation dataset. Do not claim representativeness or generalizability solely because a demonstration uses a case.

Explain necessary specialized terms at first relevant use, even when they were defined in the main manuscript. Keep standard terms when the intended readership understands them; replace unnecessary internal shorthand. A plain description such as “losses after insurance payouts” can accompany a defined outcome without changing its meaning. Keep comparator and event/period conditions needed to understand a headline finding.

Do not invent optimization objectives, agent motivations, feedback mechanisms, or numerical results to complete a template. Derive scientific motivation from the current Introduction and accepted framing. Ask only when a material gap cannot be resolved from those sources; do not demand that the user repeat an already supplied “why” before every rewrite.

## Rules Versus Preferences

No universal word or sentence cap, one-paragraph requirement, fixed opening, mandatory middle-position study area, raw numbers ban, connector ban, or punctuation ban follows from these functions. Apply verified venue requirements and explicit project/advisor preferences as classified overlays. Use numbers when they improve the main answer and are supported; retain meaningful comparisons even when numbers are omitted.

When composing another abstract-writing skill, classify its rules with [overlay-contract.md](overlay-contract.md). A legacy six-part template or banned-word list is not universal authority; do not import instructions that require unsupported claims or conflict with the active project. Count limits only against the identified current requirement. Observable stock phrasing is a readability diagnostic, never proof of AI authorship.

## Compression and Standalone Checks

After shortening either summary, compare it with the current evidence map. Check whether compression dropped a denominator, population, baseline, time window, model-only qualifier, or inference boundary. Ensure that descriptive similarity did not become equivalence, a model rule did not become a demonstrated cause, and a conditional metric did not become overall protection or vulnerability.

Read the exact Abstract alone. Can a broad disciplinary reader explain the method, research task, subject, relevant scope, main answer, and contribution? Read the Conclusion separately: does it synthesize the actual study rather than make a new claim? Recheck both after material changes elsewhere.
