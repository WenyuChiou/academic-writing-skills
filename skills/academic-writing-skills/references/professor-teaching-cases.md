## Use and Source Boundaries

Load the matching case for a requested demonstration or a writing problem that
needs one. Use the linked section guide for the governing checks. Do not apply
every case to every paragraph or require access to private source files for an
ordinary edit.

The source documents informed these teaching cases; the repairs and hypothetical
passages below are teaching paraphrases, not quotations, original manuscript
changes, or verified scientific findings. The linked examples do not certify
their numbers, citations, wording, or venue compliance. Retain only these compact
lessons and source links; do not redistribute the complete private documents or
their images. If a linked source becomes inaccessible, distinguish the retained
lesson from an unverified claim about that source's current contents.

## Paragraphs and Extended Outlines

- **Problem:** individually readable paragraphs can still repeat a function,
  introduce detail before its prerequisite, or fail to build an argument.
- **Trigger:** an outline request, a substantive paragraph/section review, or a
  reported transition problem. A sentence-level grammar request does not trigger
  reverse-outlining the section.
- **Judgment:** read the actual opening sentences in order, with adjacent
  context. Separate what the openings say from what the paragraphs actually do.
  Check whether each adds a distinct step supported by available evidence.
- **Minimal repair:** move a hidden main point into its opening; combine repeated
  functions only when authorized; restore the missing bridge rather than rewriting
  the whole section. For a new draft, propose topic sentences and evidence needs.
- **Boundary:** an opening sequence is a diagnostic, not proof that all supporting
  reasoning is correct. Preserve the article genre and requested edit scope.

**Hypothetical example, not source text.** Three paragraph openings name a field,
list its tools, and list more tools. The second paragraph later explains that the
tools place decision authority at different levels; the third adds no distinct
evidence. Record the original openings first. Then propose this progression:
the field's problem → the consequential difference between approaches → the
unresolved comparison and this paper's proposed typology. Do not report the
typology as an established result before its supporting argument exists.

Follow [lifecycle-and-routing.md](lifecycle-and-routing.md) for forward/reverse
outlining and [introduction-and-study-context.md](introduction-and-study-context.md)
for problem, gap, and study response. A conceptual paper may proceed through an
inventory, classification axes, examples, and implications; do not force a Study
Area or empirical Results section onto it.

## Abstracts with Missing Findings

- **Problem:** background dominates, several model names and processing steps
  obscure the research task, the purpose appears last, or no finding is given.
- **Trigger:** abstract drafting, substantive revision, or an independent-reader
  comprehension check.
- **Judgment:** identify what was studied, how, for whom or what setting, the
  scope needed to interpret it, and the principal contribution. Distinguish a
  proposed objective from an observed result or a demonstrated method capability.
- **Minimal repair:** compress duplicated background, move the purpose before
  implementation detail, and retain only the method information needed to follow
  the contribution. Obtain missing findings from the current manuscript or verified
  outputs; request them if neither is supplied.
- **Boundary:** do not invent a result, optimization objective, number, causal
  mechanism, or required geographic/time scope to complete a polished abstract.
  A protocol or conceptual article requires its own contribution, not fabricated
  experimental findings or a fixed sentence count.

**Hypothetical repair, not a final abstract.** Replace a long inventory of model
components with a direct study-identity sentence, such as: "This study couples a
resource-system model with a model of users' choices to examine allocation
decisions in the study basin." Use only the method and setting supported by the
actual study. Leave the findings explicitly pending in a working draft until
verified evidence is available; do not substitute "the framework improves
management" for an absent finding or call that draft submission-ready.

Follow [conclusion-and-abstract.md](conclusion-and-abstract.md) for flexible
information functions and summary-compression checks. Fixing the source example's
writing does not establish that its model was validated or its objective achieved.

## Reviewer Responses and Actual Work Status

- **Problem:** a response reports activity without answering the concern, hides
  its meaning in a figure, or claims a proposed change as completed.
- **Trigger:** response planning, drafting, or revision against a reviewer comment.
- **Judgment:** classify the actual work as new analysis, clarification of existing
  work, or a justified non-adoption. State the answer, verified evidence, meaning
  for the concern, and actual manuscript/SM destination. Use no analysis or locator
  that has not been checked in the designated files.
- **Minimal repair:** replace an ambiguous "we addressed this" with the applicable
  action below, keeping a short clarification short. Name what remained unresolved
  when evidence is unavailable.
- **Boundary:** preserve reviewer wording and distinguish proposed strategy,
  advisor-facing working draft, and verified final response. Approval of a plan
  does not establish completion or authorize additional execution.

Use these **hypothetical teaching patterns**, not claims about completed work:

| Actual situation | Response function and minimal repair |
|---|---|
| A requested comparison has been performed | Name the matched comparison and its verified result; explain what that result changes or leaves stable, then identify the actual edit or evidence destination. Do not stop at "we ran a test." |
| The reviewer misunderstood an existing procedure | Explain the procedure directly. Report a clarification only if the text was actually clarified; if unchanged wording already answers the concern, identify it as existing text rather than claiming a new method or analysis. |
| An alternative was not adopted | Give the verified reason and closest defensible response. For example, a comparison may show larger error for an alternative, but it does not validate every aspect of the retained method. Keep remaining limits visible; do not invent a rationale merely to defend a preferred result. |
| A test and manuscript edit remain pending | Label the strategy or working draft as pending and keep the concern open. Do not write "we confirmed unchanged conclusions" or insert final line numbers. A final response requires the completed evidence and artifact checks. |

When scope is narrowed instead of adding an empirical case, propagate the narrower
claim through the relevant questions, Methods, Results, and summaries. Explain
what the demonstration establishes and what it does not; a synonym change alone
does not resolve a validation concern.

Follow [reviewer-response-workflow.md](reviewer-response-workflow.md) for the ledger,
response-first handoff when applicable, inference boundaries, and promise-to-artifact
reconciliation. Its strategy/working/final states govern the examples here.

## Figures and Tables: Existing ABM Check Entries

- **Problem:** a display can look familiar while its quantity, denominator,
  uncertainty, or final-size labels remain unclear.
- **Trigger:** a display/caption/note request, a number or metric change, or
  reorganization affecting panel references. Do not redraw a figure for a
  grammar-only edit elsewhere.
- **Judgment:** select the display's reader function and verify the relevant
  quantities and encodings against available evidence. Keep reading instructions
  in captions/notes and the scientific interpretation in prose.
- **Minimal repair:** use the matching entry below rather than creating another
  caption template or copying a complete main table into SM.
- **Boundary:** existing ABM source text and images are historical examples;
  their inclusion is not a current data, reference, venue, or Word-layout audit.

Use [figures-tables-and-supplements.md](figures-tables-and-supplements.md) for these
three existing worked examples:

| Existing entry | Matching check |
|---|---|
| Original ABM Figure 1 and Maps, Borders, and Grids | Distinguish context from analysis, tract area from household counts, and zero from missing data; verify GIS provenance and inspect scale/legend/labels at final size. |
| Original ABM Table 1 and its separate teaching note | Separate parameter setting, qualitative rationale, and source; do not mistake threshold bounds for observed rates or confidence intervals. Verify actual venue borders, not nonprinting Word gridlines. |
| Original ABM Figure 6 and Reading the figure-caption design | Identify the groups, scenario, panels, quantity, denominator, statistic, and uncertainty; inspect final-size readability and reconcile figure values with prose. Do not import its years, six panels, or run count into another study. |

Retain the existing caption and note guidance as the single authority; these are
check entries, not duplicate caption rules. Use the [overlay contract](overlay-contract.md)
for current venue requirements and claim-level citation support. Mark unavailable
raw evidence or rendered layout unverified rather than certifying it from a sample.
