# CNHS, Water Policy, Framework, and Uncertainty Module

## Coupled Natural–Human Systems

Check whether the manuscript identifies the human and natural entities, state variables, decisions, feedbacks, temporal scales, spatial scales, and empirical grounding. Distinguish one-way forcing, bidirectional feedback, co-evolution, and real-time exchange.

Verify that the stated gap—empirical, conceptual, methodological, modeling, or integration—is supported by the literature. Do not assume every CNHS paper must have a modeling gap.

## Policy Evaluation

Check policy definitions, jurisdiction, legal status, implementation pathway, behavioral assumptions, scenario comparability, target timing, compliance, distributional effects, and uncertainty. Verify whether terms such as `mandatory`, `approved`, `voluntary`, or `market-based` match authoritative policy sources.

Require symmetric or explicitly justified scenario comparisons. Separate policy design, modeled implementation, behavioral response, and observed real-world feasibility.

## Framework and Tool Papers

Check requirements, prior approaches, modularity, interoperability, scalability, reuse, verification, demonstration, and adoption barriers. Require the claimed contribution to distinguish integration novelty, implementation quality, and domain insight.

Do not require a fixed survey of optimization, game theory, or ABM unless those approaches are relevant competitors for the stated problem.

## Uncertainty and Equifinality

Check definitions, parameter and structural sources, calibration or sampling methods, benchmark choices, decomposition, identifiability, uncertainty propagation, and decision relevance. Prefer `characterize`, `quantify`, `decompose`, or `reduce` to `control` only when those verbs match the analysis.

Treat classic frameworks and citations as candidates to verify, not automatic requirements. Require the manuscript to distinguish uncertainty from error and model multiplicity from empirical equivalence.

## Water-Resources Reviews and Syntheses

Check whether the synthesis method supports the stated evidence claims. Define adjacent concepts when the manuscript compares them, but do not require numbered gaps or a specific Results organization unless it serves the chosen review design.
