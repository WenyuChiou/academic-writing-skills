# Review Checklists and Evidence-Backed Cases

## Activation and Authority

Load for a supplied rubric, requested coded review, or comparison of manuscript
versions. Use the existing [round ledger](round-calibration.md) and
[overlay contract](overlay-contract.md); do not create a second severity system.
A grammar-only request does not trigger a whole-paper checklist. The cases below
are hypothetical and do not supply research facts, verified citations, or fixes
for a particular manuscript.

## Compact Check Record

For each selected criterion record: source/code, applicability and authority,
current location or artifact, evidence, coverage, issue status, and the smallest
necessary action. Coverage is `CHECKED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `OUTSIDE_SCOPE`, or
`UNVERIFIED`. A checked criterion may still have an open issue; an unavailable
artifact is not a confirmed omission. Use the round ledger's resolution states.
Keep no-change outcomes as well as findings; do not infer coverage of unread text
from a short list of violations. Reuse equivalent records and consolidate repeated
symptoms rather than producing a quota of comments or a numerical quality score.

When a material comparison or analysis changes, trace the compared entities,
observation unit, measure or denominator, aggregation, test, and supported claim
in the existing record. Select relevant links, including pairing or selection
when applicable; this is not a new form or a checklist for every sentence.

### Conditional group-code mapping

When the supplied Ethan-style rubric uses these codes, the following are concise
check entries, not a replacement for the supplied source. Apply only relevant
entries and distinguish actual requirements from preferences.

| Source codes | Check entry | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| G1, G2, G4, A1 | Stable terms; needed acronym expansion and conceptual meaning at the reading boundary | Do not redefine basic terms or rotate technical synonyms. |
| G6-G8 | Readable display; adequate caption/legend/notes; mapped panels | Inspect the display together with its caption, not caption length alone. |
| G9, G11 | Applicable page/line numbering and cross-file item ordering | Use the actual submission requirements and rendered artifact. |
| I1-I3, A2, R2 | Gap, objective, method, evidence, and substantive answer agree | Preserve conceptual/methods-paper contributions and legitimate dependent questions. |
| M2, M3 | Procedures precede the inference that needs them; notation is consistent | An equation followed immediately by its where-clause is not automatically defective. |
| R3-R5 | Displays add distinct information; main findings are accessible and coherently located | Secondary diagnostics may stay in SM; multiple visuals may answer one question. |
| D2 | Relevant design, data, model, and validation boundaries are stated | Do not recast fixable errors as limitations or demand every limitation type. |
| F2 | Bibliographic identity and claim support are checked separately | Unverified access is not proof of a fabricated reference. |

G3/G5 wording bans, G10 line targets, A3 sentence/move counts, I4/I5 paragraph
positions, and C1 conclusion templates remain source-bound preferences unless
the current author/project or venue makes them requirements. Do not infer review
round or readiness from comment count, suppress new validity issues in late
rounds, impersonate a reviewer, or forbid table notes without an applicable rule.

## Cases for Cross-Round Review and Contribution

### 1. Relocation is not the requested clarification

- **Problem:** a reviewer requests the population and denominator for a treatment
  uptake percentage. The author moves its paragraph to Methods and replies `fixed`,
  but the denominator remains unstated.
- **Trigger:** compare the requested reader function with the current artifact.
- **Judgment:** moving text is not evidence that its meaning was clarified.
- **Minimal action:** request the verified denominator and add it where needed;
  check related displays. Record `OPEN`, or `PARTIAL` if relocation itself also
  satisfied an explicit part of the request.
- **Boundary:** do not prescribe wholesale rewriting when relocation alone actually
  resolves the requested ordering or context problem.

### 2. No reply does not mean no repair

- **Problem:** a comment asks what a habitat score measures. There is no comment
  reply, but the new Methods defines the score and the current caption uses it
  consistently.
- **Trigger:** a missing reply is mistaken for an unresolved manuscript issue.
- **Judgment:** verify the substantive repair independently of thread activity.
- **Minimal action:** record `RESOLVED` for the manuscript; request a reply separately
  only when that deliverable is required.
- **Boundary:** checking the caption text does not establish plotted-value accuracy
  without the required display or source output. For a suspected digit or sign
  mismatch in a downscaled image, inspect the original-resolution mark and
  distinguish a printed sign from a nearby line before reporting a numerical error.
  If that inspection is unavailable or inconclusive, record it as unverified.

### 3. A known input difference is not the whole contribution

- **Problem:** a simulation assigns group A more assets than group B, then presents
  A's larger absolute loss as the main new finding without further analysis.
- **Trigger:** the claimed contribution may be a direct consequence of setup.
- **Judgment:** ask what the analysis establishes beyond the known input contrast:
  magnitude, an appropriate comparison, heterogeneity, uncertainty, or a boundary.
- **Minimal action:** identify the additional insight in existing evidence, or narrow
  the claim if none is provided. Do not invent a normalization or new result.
- **Boundary:** expected findings can be useful; a methods paper may contribute
  evaluation or implementation, and no study must produce a surprising result.

### 4. A behavioral measure is not automatically a physical outcome

- **Problem:** a survey reports willingness to conserve water; the summary calls
  this demonstrated water savings.
- **Trigger:** a compressed conclusion changes the measured outcome.
- **Judgment:** willingness, action, and physical effect are separate claims.
- **Minimal action:** restore the measured outcome and recheck affected summaries;
  request additional evidence only if the stronger claim must be retained.
- **Boundary:** retain an actual measured physical outcome when it is supplied.

### 5. Screening can change the retained population

- **Problem:** invalid generated records are excluded more often in one group,
  but the reported comparison still claims to represent all intended profiles.
- **Trigger:** generation, retry, parsing, filtering, or missing-data rules can
  change which observations enter the analysis.
- **Judgment:** trace attempted records through exclusions to retained records
  and the comparison's actual population; unequal retention alone does not
  establish the direction or magnitude of selection bias.
- **Minimal action:** request unavailable screening evidence or narrow the claim
  to the documented retained population; check affected summaries and displays.
- **Boundary:** preserve a transparent, appropriately scoped retained-record
  comparison. Do not demand equal group sizes, invent missing counts, or require
  new experiments merely because exclusions occurred.

### 6. Nondetection is not equivalence; supported equivalence is not a defect

- **Problem:** a nonsignificant difference is described as proof that two methods
  are equivalent, without an equivalence criterion or supporting analysis.
- **Trigger:** a claim of sameness, equivalence, agreement, or an unchanged result
  is stronger than the supplied comparison supports.
- **Judgment:** identify the quantity compared and the evidence needed for that
  specific claim. Distinguish nondetection from equivalence within justified bounds.
- **Minimal action:** narrow an unsupported claim; retain an appropriately
  justified equivalence conclusion within its metric, bounds, and study scope.
- **Boundary:** do not ban `similar` or `equivalent`, force a particular test, or
  infer that equivalence on one outcome means the methods are identical overall.

## Comparison and Test Reporting

For V1/V2 work, identify exact files and the accepted-text/markup view used. Map
related passages by meaning, not paragraph number alone. Separate historical
repairs, remaining issues, new regressions, and unavailable evidence; do not call
every difference an improvement or attribute an author edit to the skill.

Use these cases to design fresh, cross-domain probes with both defects and
no-change controls. Test actual model decisions separately from route/link checks.
A passing static test or one successful manuscript review does not establish a
general detection rate, causal skill improvement, or submission readiness.
