# Flood, Hydrodynamics, and Catastrophe Review Module

## Trigger and Scope

Use for flood hazard or risk, rainfall–runoff and inundation chains, hydrodynamics, drainage or pipe–surface coupling, catastrophe models, exposure, vulnerability, insurance, adaptation, or loss. Load computational, psychometric, or CNHS modules when those components support separate claims.

## Study Context and Model Setup

Distinguish geographic and hazard context from reproducible setup. Context may include watershed, drainage, historical events, exposure, and observations. Methods should explain input preparation, boundary and initial conditions, parameters, calibration, validation, scenarios, and execution.

Classify passages by reader function rather than moving them mechanically by keywords.

## Hydrology, Hydrodynamics, and Drainage

Check forcing, boundary conditions, spatial and temporal resolution, calibration and validation events, conservation behavior, roughness or hydraulic parameters, pipe–surface exchange, one-way versus two-way coupling, exchanged variables, and update frequency.

Use rainfall, discharge, water depth, water surface elevation, surcharge, and inundation terms precisely. Verify units, vertical references, event counts, and every claimed model capability against Methods and figures.

## Catastrophe and Flood-Risk Models

Trace hazard → exposure → vulnerability → ground-up loss → insured, financial, behavioral, or policy outcome. Check event definition, aggregation, baseline, adaptation and insurance logic, uncertainty, validation evidence, and whether modeled loss is confused with observed loss.

For household or agent behavior, verify the mapping between social groups, tenure, feasible actions, exposure, and financial outcomes. Do not treat a direct consequence of an input assumption as an emergent mechanism.

## Figures, Maps, and Events

Check panel completeness, event and model counts, normalization, spatial extent, resolution, color scales, units, labels, uncertainty, readability, and whether captions distinguish observed, simulated, and derived quantities. Verify modified-source attribution against venue and license requirements.

Explain each figure's main pattern and evidence. Require a physical or behavioral mechanism only when analysis or cited evidence supports it.

## Claim and Language Boundaries

Use `prediction` for genuine forecasting or predictive evaluation and `simulation` for process-model scenario output. Verify superiority claims with the relevant metric, event range, uncertainty, and use case.

Do not infer reduced real-world risk from lower modeled loss without preserving the model, scenario, exposure, adaptation, and financial assumptions that produce the result.
