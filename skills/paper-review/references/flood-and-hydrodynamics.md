# Flood, Catastrophe, and Hydrodynamics Module

## Study Context and Model Setup

Distinguish geographic and hazard context from reproducible model setup. Context may include location, watershed or drainage characteristics, historical events, and available observations. Methods should explain input preparation, boundary conditions, parameters, calibration, validation, scenarios, and model execution.

Do not move text mechanically by keywords. Classify each passage by reader function and manuscript structure.

## Hydrodynamic and Drainage Coupling

Check one-way versus two-way exchange, exchanged variables, temporal frequency, boundary conditions, pipe–surface interaction, spatial resolution, and conservation behavior. Verify every architecture arrow and claimed model capability against Methods and figures.

Use water depth, water surface elevation, discharge, surcharge, and inundation terms precisely. Confirm units and vertical references.

## Catastrophe and Flood-Risk Models

Trace hazard → exposure → vulnerability → loss → financial or behavioral outcome. Check event definitions, aggregation, baseline, insurance or adaptation logic, uncertainty, validation evidence, and whether modeled loss is confused with observed loss.

## Figures and Events

Check panel completeness, event counts, normalization, spatial extent, color scales, labels, readability, and whether captions distinguish observed, simulated, and derived quantities. Require modified-source attribution when applicable to the venue and license.

## Domain-Specific Language

Use `prediction` for genuine forecasting or predictive evaluation and `simulation` for process-model scenario output. Do not impose either term globally.

Verify claims about model superiority with the relevant metric, event range, uncertainty, and use case. If a mechanism is proposed for performance differences, require analysis or cited physical evidence.
