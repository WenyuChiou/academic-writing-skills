# Quantitative, Psychometrics, and SEM Review Module

## Trigger and Scope

Use for surveys, scales, questionnaires, behavioral constructs, psychometrics, EFA, CFA, latent-variable models, SEM, mediation, moderation, or multi-group analysis. Do not load it for every paper that reports a mean or regression.

## Sampling and Estimand

Check sampling frame, inclusion and exclusion, missingness, weights, clustering, pairing, repeated observations, subgroup size, measurement timing, multiplicity, effect sizes, and uncertainty. Identify the estimand before selecting or judging a test.

Do not assume unequal sample size requires an independent-sample test. Determine whether observations are independent, paired, nested, weighted, clustered, or repeated. Separate within-group significance from a direct between-group test.

## Measurement and Psychometrics

Check construct definition, item source and adaptation, response scale, translation when relevant, scoring, reverse coding, missing-item rules, distribution, reliability, and validity evidence. Reliability alone does not establish validity, and a high coefficient does not prove unidimensionality.

For factor models, check estimator choice for the item scale, identification, sample adequacy, loadings, residuals, factor correlations, cross-loadings or correlated errors, fit evidence, theory behind modifications, and sensitivity to retained items. Treat common cutoffs as context-dependent guidance rather than universal pass–fail laws.

Distinguish exploratory item reduction from confirmatory evaluation. Report when item removal changes the construct's content domain. Check convergent, discriminant, criterion, and known-groups evidence only when the manuscript claims them.

For group comparisons of latent constructs, check configural, metric, scalar, and residual invariance to the degree required by the intended comparison. Do not compare latent means or structural paths as formally different without the appropriate invariance and constraint tests.

## SEM and Path Models

Separate the measurement model from the structural model. Check model specification, identification, estimator, missing-data treatment, standardized versus unstandardized estimates, direct and indirect effects, uncertainty, model fit, alternative models, and whether causal language exceeds the design.

For mediation, check temporal and causal assumptions and use appropriate uncertainty for indirect effects. For multi-group analysis, require a formal constrained comparison for claims of path differences; one significant path and one nonsignificant path do not establish a significant difference.

Poor reliability or measurement fit does not automatically prohibit descriptive reporting, but it narrows construct-level and structural claims. State that boundary explicitly.

## Results, Tables, and Claims

Trace every reported coefficient, test, sample count, and fit statistic to the correct model and subgroup. Verify signs, reference groups, units, rounding, confidence intervals, p-values, and table or figure labels.

Use `reliability and validity assessment` when that is the actual task. Reserve `validated`, `equivalent`, `reproduced`, and `invariant` for analyses and criteria that support those claims.
