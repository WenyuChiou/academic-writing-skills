# Water Resources, CNHS, Policy, and Uncertainty Review Module

## Trigger and Scope

Use for water-resources systems, hydrology-linked human systems, coupled natural–human systems, water policy, framework or tool papers, water reviews, model uncertainty, or equifinality. Load the flood module for flood, inundation, drainage, catastrophe, or hydrodynamic claims.

## Coupled Natural–Human Systems

Check whether the manuscript identifies natural, human, and institutional entities; state variables; decisions; feedbacks; spatial and temporal scales; empirical grounding; and exchanged information. Distinguish one-way forcing, bidirectional feedback, co-evolution, and real-time exchange.

Verify whether the gap is empirical, conceptual, methodological, modeling, or integration-based. Do not assume every CNHS or ABM paper has a modeling gap.

## Water Policy and Decision Support

Check policy definitions, jurisdiction, legal status, implementation pathway, behavioral assumptions, timing, compliance, distributional effects, target and baseline comparability, and uncertainty. Verify terms such as `mandatory`, `approved`, `voluntary`, or `market-based` against authoritative sources.

Separate policy design, modeled implementation, behavioral response, scenario result, and observed real-world feasibility. Require symmetric comparisons or an explicit reason for asymmetry.

## Framework and Tool Papers

Check requirements, prior approaches, modularity, interoperability, scalability, reuse, verification, demonstration, and adoption barriers. Distinguish integration novelty, implementation quality, methodological contribution, and domain insight.

Do not require a fixed survey of optimization, game theory, or ABM unless those approaches are relevant alternatives for the stated problem.

## Uncertainty and Equifinality

Check definitions, parameter and structural sources, data and observation uncertainty, calibration or sampling method, benchmark, identifiability, sensitivity, uncertainty propagation, decomposition, and decision relevance.

Distinguish uncertainty from error, equifinality from empirical equivalence, and scenario spread from probabilistic prediction. Use `characterize`, `quantify`, `decompose`, or `reduce` only when they match the analysis; do not prescribe a verb by habit.

## Water Reviews and Syntheses

Check protocol, source selection, screening, coding, concept definitions, synthesis logic, evidence traceability, heterogeneity, and claim scope. Organize findings around the paper's questions or synthesis design rather than enforcing a fixed thematic structure.
